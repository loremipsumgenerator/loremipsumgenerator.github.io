Placeholder Text in Mockups

Make your mockups look real without writing final copy. Practical rules for length, labelling and client reviews.

Why mockups need filler

A mockup without text looks empty, and an empty mockup is hard to judge. Clients cannot tell whether a layout works if the spaces where words will go are blank, because the eye reads emptiness as a design choice. Placeholder text fills those spaces with realistic texture, so the client can see the balance of the page, the rhythm of headings and paragraphs, and the weight of the typography. It turns an abstract grid into something that feels like a page.

Matching lengths to reality

The most important skill in mockup filler is length. A paragraph of lorem ipsum should be about as long as the real paragraph will be, and a headline should have about as many words as the real headline. If you fill a card with eighty words when the real content will be twenty, the client approves a design that will look empty in reality. Ask the copywriter or client for approximate lengths early, and generate filler to match.

Labelling placeholders clearly

Always make it obvious that the text is a placeholder. A small note in the corner of the mockup, a different colour for draft text, or a caption that says 'sample text' all work. Non-designers often do not know what lorem ipsum is, and they may think it is a mistake or a foreign language. A clear label prevents confused emails and keeps the review focused on the design.

High-fidelity vs low-fidelity

In low-fidelity wireframes, grey bars or single lines can stand in for text, because the question is structure, not appearance. In high-fidelity mockups, real-looking placeholder text is better, because the question is how the finished page will feel. Match the fidelity of your filler to the fidelity of the mockup: grey bars in a polished mockup look unfinished, and full paragraphs in a rough sketch waste time.

Moving from mockup to real content

Plan the handover from placeholder to real copy early. Give the copywriter the mockup with notes on the length and purpose of each block, and set a deadline for the real text before development starts. When the real copy arrives, check every block against the design: some will fit, some will need the layout adjusted, and a few will need rewriting. Budget time for that adjustment; it is a normal part of the process, not a failure.

Mockups for developers

Developers use mockups as a specification, so the placeholder text in a mockup tells them what kind of content to expect. A paragraph of lorem ipsum in a card tells the developer that the card holds a paragraph, but not how long it will be or what it will say. Add a note to the mockup with the expected length and the field name from the database, and the developer can build a component that handles the real content gracefully. Without that note, the developer guesses, and guesses cause bugs that appear only when real content arrives.

Frequently asked questions

How long should placeholder paragraphs be?

About as long as the real content will be. Ask for approximate word counts before you fill the mockup.

Should I label lorem ipsum in mockups?

Yes. Non-designers may not recognize it, so a small 'sample text' note prevents confusion.

Are grey bars better than lorem ipsum?

For rough wireframes, yes. For polished mockups, real-looking placeholder text shows the design better.

What should I tell developers about placeholder text?

Give them the field name, the expected length, and an example of real content, so they can build components that handle it properly.
