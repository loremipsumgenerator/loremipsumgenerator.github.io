Lorem Ipsum in Email Templates

Email design has tight limits. Learn how placeholder text helps you test templates, subject lines and mobile rendering.

Why email needs filler

Email templates are built before the copy is ready, just like websites, but email has tighter constraints: narrow columns, limited fonts, and clients that render HTML differently. Placeholder text lets the designer test a template with realistic content before the first real campaign is written. It shows whether the header is too tall, the call to action is visible without scrolling, and the footer fits on a phone screen.

Testing on mobile

Most emails are now read on phones, so test your template with placeholder text on a small screen. Generate a paragraph and see how it reflows: does the line length stay comfortable, do the images scale, and does the button stay tappable? Test with the longest realistic subject line and preheader too, because a long subject can push the content down and hide the call to action. Mobile testing with filler catches problems that a desktop preview misses.

Subject lines and preheaders

Placeholder text is not suitable for subject lines, because the subject is the most important copy in an email and it must be real to test properly. Instead, use a realistic example subject and preheader, even if they are drafts, and test how they look in different inboxes. A subject that is cut off on a phone, or a preheader that repeats the subject, is a problem that only real words reveal.

Images, alt text and filler

Email templates often use placeholder images alongside placeholder text. Remember that many email clients block images by default, so the alt text is what the reader sees first. Write realistic alt text even in a template, and test the email with images blocked to see whether it still makes sense. A template that works with images off and placeholder text on is robust; one that falls apart is not ready.

Before you send

Never send a real campaign with placeholder text in it. Before any send, search the template for 'lorem' and 'ipsum', check every block against the final copy, and send a test email to yourself and a colleague. Check the test on a phone and a desktop, with images on and off. This takes five minutes and prevents the embarrassing mistake of sending thousands of subscribers an email full of Latin.

Dark mode in email

Many email clients now support dark mode, which inverts the colors of your template. Placeholder text helps you test this: send a test email with filler, open it in dark mode, and check that the text is still readable and the images still make sense. A logo designed for a white background may disappear on a dark one, and light grey text may become invisible. Test every template in both light and dark mode before sending, and provide a dark-mode version of your logo if needed.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use lorem ipsum in email templates?

Yes, while designing and testing the template. Replace it with real copy before any campaign is sent.

Should I test email templates on mobile?

Always. Most emails are read on phones, and filler text shows how the layout reflows on a small screen.

Can I use placeholder text for subject lines?

No. Subject lines need realistic draft copy to test properly, because length and wording matter so much.

Do I need to test emails in dark mode?

Yes. Many clients invert template colors, so check that text stays readable and logos remain visible in both modes.
