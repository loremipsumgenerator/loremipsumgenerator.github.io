Generating Lorem Ipsum by Words, Sentences and Paragraphs

Words, sentences or paragraphs: which unit should you generate, and how much do you need for each kind of layout?

Words for small spaces

Generate by words when the space is small and exact: a button label, a card title, a caption, or a test of how text wraps. Ten words show whether a headline breaks awkwardly, fifty words show how a card fills, and a hundred words test a narrow column. Word counts are also useful when a client asks how much copy a space holds: generate the exact number of words the space fits and show them what that looks like.

Sentences for rhythm

Generate by sentences when you need realistic rhythm: a headline plus a subheading, a testimonial, or a short introduction. Sentences show how punctuation and varied lengths affect the design, which a block of words cannot. Three to five sentences make a good introduction, one sentence tests a quote, and ten sentences test a short article section. Sentence generation is the best choice when the shape of the text matters more than its total length.

Paragraphs for full layouts

Generate by paragraphs when you are filling a whole page or article: a blog post, a landing page, or a report. Paragraphs show the vertical rhythm of a page, the spacing between blocks, and how headings break up long text. One paragraph tests a card, three test a section, and five or more test a full article. Always generate at least one more paragraph than you think you need, because real content usually grows.

Matching the unit to the design

The rule is simple: match the unit to the element. Buttons and labels need words, quotes and introductions need sentences, and pages and articles need paragraphs. Mixing units in one mockup is fine, as long as each block uses the unit that matches its real content. A page where every block is three identical paragraphs looks fake; a page with varied blocks looks like a real page.

How much is enough?

For a quick layout test, one paragraph is enough. For a client review, fill the whole page with realistic amounts, because empty sections invite the client to imagine the worst. For a template, generate the maximum realistic amount for each block, so buyers see the template at its fullest. And always keep a copy of the amounts you used, so the copywriter knows the target length for each block.

Counting words in real copy

When you brief a copywriter, give them a word count for each block, based on the amounts you tested with placeholder text. A brief that says 'introduction, 50 words' is far more useful than one that says 'write an introduction'. The copywriter knows exactly how much space they have, and the designer knows the copy will fit. Keep a record of the word counts for every block in the project, and update it when the design changes, so the brief always matches the layout.

Frequently asked questions

Should I generate words, sentences or paragraphs?

Match the unit to the element: words for labels, sentences for quotes and intros, paragraphs for pages and articles.

How many paragraphs for a blog mockup?

Three to five paragraphs with headings shows the rhythm of a real article. Add more for long-form layouts.

Why do my mockups look fake?

Often because every block is the same length. Vary the amounts so the page looks like real content.

How do I brief a copywriter on length?

Give a word count for each block, based on the placeholder amounts you tested. Update the counts whenever the design changes.
