Lorem Ipsum for Client Presentations

How to present designs with placeholder text without confusing the client — or losing the sale.

Set expectations before the meeting

The most important step happens before you open the mockup: tell the client that the text is a placeholder. A short sentence in the meeting invite or the first slide, such as 'The text in these designs is sample text and will be replaced with your final copy', prevents the most common confusion. Clients who know what to expect judge the design; clients who do not know spend the meeting asking why the site is in Latin.

Choosing filler for the audience

Match the filler to the client. For a design-savvy client, classic lorem ipsum is fine and even reassuring, because it signals professionalism. For a client who has never seen a mockup, plain English filler is better, because they can see that it is a stand-in without needing an explanation. For a formal client, avoid themed or funny generators entirely; they undermine the seriousness of the presentation.

Talking about the design, not the words

During the presentation, keep the focus on the design. Point to the layout, the hierarchy, the spacing and the images, and say explicitly that the words are not the subject of this review. If a client starts editing the placeholder, gently redirect them: 'That text will be replaced; today I would love your thoughts on the layout.' A confident redirect keeps the meeting productive.

When to replace filler before presenting

For the most important screens, such as the homepage hero, replace the placeholder with draft copy before the presentation. A real headline makes the design feel finished and helps the client imagine the final product. You do not need real copy everywhere; one or two key screens with realistic words, and placeholder elsewhere, gives the client the best of both worlds.

After the presentation

Send the client a summary that notes which text is placeholder and when the real copy is due. Include a list of the content you need from them, with lengths and deadlines. This turns the presentation into a clear next step instead of a vague approval, and it prevents the common problem of a client approving a design and then delivering copy that does not fit it.

Presenting to non-technical clients

Non-technical clients often need more explanation than designers expect. They may not know what a wireframe is, what a mockup is, or why the text is in Latin. Start the presentation with a one-minute explanation of the process: this is a draft, the text is a stand-in, and today we are deciding the layout. Use plain language, avoid jargon, and check understanding as you go. A client who understands the process gives better feedback and trusts the designer more.

Frequently asked questions

Should I explain lorem ipsum to clients?

Yes, briefly, before the meeting. One sentence is enough: the text is a sample and will be replaced with final copy.

Is English filler better than Latin for clients?

Often yes, especially for clients who have never seen a mockup. It is immediately clear that it is a stand-in.

What if the client edits the placeholder text?

Thank them, note the feedback for the real copy, and redirect the conversation to the layout, which is the purpose of the review.

How much should I explain to a non-technical client?

A one-minute plain-language explanation at the start is enough: this is a draft, the text is a stand-in, and today we decide the layout.
