Placeholder Text for Mobile Apps

Small screens, short attention spans: how to use placeholder text when designing mobile apps and testing tiny layouts.

Why mobile needs different filler

Mobile screens are small, so every word counts more. A paragraph that looks fine on a desktop can overwhelm a phone screen, and a headline that fits on a wide screen can break across four lines on a phone. Placeholder text for mobile should be generated in the exact amounts the app will use: short labels, one-line descriptions, and paragraphs of no more than three sentences. Test with the longest realistic content, because mobile layouts break first.

Cards, lists and short blocks

Most mobile apps are built from cards and lists, each with a small amount of text. Generate short blocks of filler for these: a title of three to five words, a description of ten to fifteen words, and a price or date. Vary the lengths between cards so the list looks realistic, and check that the cards stay aligned when one description is longer than the others. A list where every card has identical filler hides alignment problems.

Testing with real device sizes

Always test mobile placeholder text on a real device or an accurate simulator, not just in a desktop browser. A phone's font rendering, line breaks and touch targets differ from a desktop preview, and a layout that looks fine in a browser can feel cramped in the hand. Generate filler, load it in the prototype, and hold the phone at a normal distance: can you read it comfortably, and can you tap the buttons without hitting the wrong one?

Empty states and loading screens

Mobile apps have empty states, such as a blank inbox or a list with no items, and loading screens, where content is still arriving. Placeholder text plays a role here too: skeleton screens use grey bars to suggest where text will appear, and empty states need a short, friendly message, not lorem ipsum. Write real copy for empty states from the start, because they are part of the product, not a layout problem.

From placeholder to production

Before a mobile app ships, every screen must be checked for placeholder text, including screens that are rarely seen, such as error messages, settings and onboarding. Search the project for 'lorem' and 'ipsum', and check each screen on a real device with real content. Mobile users are unforgiving of unfinished-looking apps, and a single screen of Latin in an otherwise polished app can earn a one-star review.

Placeholder text and app store screenshots

App store screenshots are often made from mockups, and a screenshot that contains lorem ipsum will be rejected by reviewers or ignored by users. Always use real, polished copy in store screenshots, even if the app itself is still in development. The screenshots are marketing, not design drafts, and they must show the app at its best. Plan the copy for screenshots as a separate task, with the same care as the app's real content.

Frequently asked questions

How much placeholder text fits on a phone?

Very little. Use short blocks: titles of 3 to 5 words and descriptions of 10 to 15 words for cards and lists.

Should I use skeleton screens instead of lorem ipsum?

For loading states, yes. Grey bars suggest where content will appear without showing fake words.

Can placeholder text reach a shipped app?

It should never. Search the project for 'lorem' before release and check every screen, including error and settings screens.

Can I use lorem ipsum in app store screenshots?

No. Store screenshots are marketing and must show real, polished copy, even if the app is still in development.
