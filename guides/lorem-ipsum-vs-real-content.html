Lorem Ipsum vs Real Content

Placeholder text hides real problems. Learn how design changes when real copy arrives, and how to plan for it.

What placeholder hides

Lorem ipsum is evenly textured: the words are similar in length, the sentences are similar in rhythm, and nothing stands out. Real copy is lumpy: it has short punchy sentences, long explanations, names, numbers, quotes and calls to action. A layout that looks balanced with placeholder text can fall apart with real copy, because a three-word heading and a fifteen-word heading need different treatment. The sooner real copy enters the process, the fewer surprises at the end.

Length is the biggest surprise

Real copy is rarely the length the designer guessed. Product names are longer than expected, legal disclaimers are shorter, and testimonials ramble. Design for a range, not a single length: test your layout with the shortest and longest realistic versions of each block, and make sure both look good. A card that works with twenty words and with eighty words is robust; a card that only works with fifty is fragile.

Meaning changes the design

Real words carry meaning, and meaning changes how people read a page. A heading that asks a question needs different spacing from a heading that makes a statement. A warning needs a different treatment from a tip. Placeholder text cannot show these differences, because it means nothing. Once real copy exists, review the design again with fresh eyes and adjust emphasis, spacing and hierarchy to match what the words actually say.

The content-first argument

Some designers argue for content-first design: write the copy before designing anything, and let the words shape the layout. It produces honest, effective pages, because the design serves the message rather than the other way round. It is not always practical, since copy often arrives late, but even a rough draft written early is better than Latin. If you can get any real words before you start, take them.

A practical hybrid workflow

In practice, most projects use a hybrid: placeholder text for early layout, real draft copy as soon as it exists, and final copy before development. Set a date for the draft copy, and treat it as a milestone, not an afterthought. When the draft arrives, do a full design pass with the real words, and budget a day for the adjustments. The result is a design that fits its content, not one that merely looked good with filler.

Real content and search rankings

Real content also matters for search engines, which cannot rank a page that has no meaningful words. A page designed with placeholder text and then filled with thin, rushed copy will rank poorly, however beautiful the layout. Plan the copy with the same care as the design: research what visitors are searching for, answer their questions clearly, and write headings that describe the content. A design that fits its real content, and content that deserves its design, is the combination that performs well for both people and search engines.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my design break with real copy?

Real copy varies in length and structure far more than placeholder text. Test with the shortest and longest realistic versions of each block.

Should I design content-first?

If you can. Writing copy first lets the design serve the message. If copy arrives late, use placeholder text early and switch as soon as drafts exist.

Can I use lorem ipsum in the final design?

No. It is a stand-in for layout work only. Every live page needs real, meaningful copy.

Does real copy help SEO?

Yes. Search engines rank pages on their content, so a page with real, helpful copy will always outperform a page built around filler.
